## Why it mattered

Job postings are long and inconsistent, and what a job asks for (education, experience, skills, certifications) is buried inside them. Downstream classifiers need those sections cut out first.

## Problem

A single LLM pass over every posting would be too expensive and too slow at this scale.

## Constraints

A fixed GPU budget, a throughput target, and a small labelling budget.

## Approach

**Pretraining.** I ran domain-adaptive pretraining across <Metric id="dapt-encoders" /> encoder architectures, with an agent-driven experiment loop and automated hyperparameter search. I compared the models on bits-per-byte, which does not depend on the tokenizer, and chose the ModernBERT checkpoint that became the backbone of the stack.

**Serving.** I built the serving path on RTX 5090s: FP8 quantisation, FlashAttention-2 and minimal-padding batching.

**Extraction.** A cascade in three tiers: rules first, then a fine-tuned encoder, then an LLM for what the first two leave. The encoder is a ModernBERT NER model trained by teacher-student distillation: an LLM teacher labelled a weak-supervision-stratified sample of postings, and the smaller student learned from those labels. It segments each posting into <Metric id="ner-section-types" /> section types (education, experience, skills, certifications, responsibilities and more). Interns labelled a gold set and I checked it.<WhenReady ids="ner-f1"> The model reaches <Metric id="ner-f1" /> against it.</WhenReady>

**Education predictions.** I built the education classifiers that read the extracted sections.

## Result

<Metric id="edu-predictions" /> education predictions shipped, which I validated with an LLM acting as judge.
